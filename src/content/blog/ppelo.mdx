Everybody wants to sell you something. You ignore that truth to your own detriment.

If you are an adult chess improver and have spent money on improvement products (coaching, chessable courses, training platforms, books, etc), I'd like you to total the elo points you've gained since you started purchasing those products, total the amount of money you have spent on those products, and then calculate the price per elo point gained.

$.50 per elo point? $1, $3, $10? more?

Don't worry, you don't have to tell anyone. If you are like me, you'd be too embarassed to say. But you should think about it. I do. And it should inform how you approach chess improvement. 


I imagine that in at least one way the chess industry is similar to the boxing industry: only elite players make real money. Real Money, of course, means something quite different to Floyd Mayweather than it does to Magnus Carlsen.

Regardless of what <i>Real Money</i> means, nonelite chess players, if they make money from chess at all, likely make it from selling chess improvement products or services. Who has money to pay for those products and services? Adult chess improvers. As a general rule, the older the more disposable income the more purchasing power. Also true is that the older the more difficult it is to improve.

I believe that economic truth is what drives the enomous amount of online content about chess improvement for adults. You can't escape it on YouTube. And just about every YouTuber who regularly posts content about chess improvement has a product to sell you. Now, mind you, I find nothing wrong with that. Creating free content with the goal of converting a percentage itsconsumers into paying customers predates the internet. Many how-to magazine articles have been written by folks with a service to sell. 

That it is normal and that it is generally ethical (there are many exceptions to the ethical qualification) doesn't change that the content providers have an economic incentive. That Grandmaster, International Master, or Fide master has a course to sell. That non-titled YouTuber doing a speed run has a Patreon account, a paid newsletter, a paid Twitch subscription. 

Let's revisit your $ per elo calculation. How did you hear about the products you paid for? What made you feel confident in making the purchase? For the vast majority of adult chess improvers, there is a social media component--likely a YouTube content creator.

And what did you purchase? A $99 Chessable course? A $150 annual subscription to a learning platform? Your favority YouTuber's $395 Lifetime Access Opening Course?

There is no reason to be ashamed about puchasing such a product or service. But don't fool yourself. In you are starting your chess improvement journey in your forties, fifties, or even sixties, you are not going to become a Fide Master, much less an International Master. Heck, if you are in that age group and are starting at a 1000 chess.com Rapid rating, you aren't even going to get to a 2100 chess.com Rapid rating. Probably not even a 1900 chess.com Rapid Rating. 

And that is the ugly underside to the chess improvement industry. It is based on the lie that adults can get good at chess. We can't. You can't. Can we get better? Absolutely! But if you are just starting your chess improvement journey in your fifties? Forget about it! You have a hard cap. I don't know what your cap is. Nor do you. That uncertainty helps feed the lie that fuels the adult chess improver industry. 

If you are starting to play golf in your forties, you aren't going to make the PGA no matter how much you spend on golf products, coaching, or other improvement services. 

Let's do a thought experiement. Let's say are 45 years old and you played chess a bit as a child. You also played a little throughout the years, but younever competed seriously. Let's also assume you have started playing on Chess.com and have reached your natural plateau of 1250 Rapid. That is, you reached a 1250 Rapid rating just by playing, watching some free YouTube content, reading some articles, chatting on Reddit. And let's say it took you between 6 months and a year to reach 1250. 

What is your upper limit? 1800 chess.com Rapid? 2000 Chess.com Rapid? Oh, you are special and you think you can reach 2250. Fine. Pick a number. Now, subtract your current elo rating from that number. Let's say your upper limit is 2000 and your current rating is 1200. That comes to 800 elo points for you to gain. 

You are going to have to put in thousands of hours to climb those 800 points. And they have to be quality hours mind you. You can play 3 minute blitz games for 10,000 hours and not get much better at chess. Those will have to be thousands of hours spent playing "serious" chess, analyzing your games, doing challenging tactics work, and gaining skills in basic endgames. Let's say it will take you 3,200 hours to go from 1200 to 2000. That comes to 4 hours of serious chess work for each elo point of gain.

Let's ignore the economic opportunity cost of those 3,200 hours. How much are you willing to pay out of pocket for each elo point of gain? $.10, $1, $10? If your budget is $1 per elo point, that is just a handful of chessable courses. And there is no way of knowing how many elo points, if any, you'll gain by working through any course. Chess improvement courses don't come with a price per unit like some groceries do.

But there is chess content that has proven the test of time. And guess what? It is relatively inexpensive!

The [Steps Method](https://www.chess-steps.com) has a proven track record in helping chess players (albeit mostly younger players) go all the way up to 2100 Fide. The cost? Step 2 (which GM Noel Studer says is for anyone 1000-1500 Chesscom Rapid) costs 17.95 for the Manual,  $9.95 for the Step 2 Workbook, and another $9.95 for the Step 2 Workbook Extra. All in, $38.

[Silman's Complete Endgame Course](https://www.amazon.com/Silmans-Complete-Endgame-Course-Beginner/dp/1890085103) on Amazon for $22 is all you need for Endgame knowledge. 

A small number of relatively inexpensive books will suffice for strategy. [Logical Chess Move by Move](https://www.amazon.com/Logical-Chess-Move-Every-Explained/dp/1837330565).
